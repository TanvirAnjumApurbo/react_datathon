# REACT 2026 Datathon (IEEE SEU SB) — Temporal Fraud Detection: Dataset Identification & Best-Practice Winning Pipeline

## TL;DR
- **The exact REACT 2026 dataset is NOT publicly available anywhere** (not on Kaggle Datasets, GitHub, HuggingFace, Zenodo, UCI, IEEE DataPort, Mendeley, or Papers With Code). It is a **privately generated synthetic dataset** — the future-dated 2026 timestamps, the microsecond artifact in the test max, BDT amounts, the "we won't tell you how fraud was generated" framing, and the exact 13-column schema match no known public dataset. The closest *structural* analog is the **ULB Fraud-Detection-Handbook simulator** (raw customer/terminal/timestamp/amount schema), followed by **IEEE-CIS (Vesta)** and **Sparkov**.
- **Because the metric is Average Precision (PR-AUC) with ~1.5–2% fraud and a strict future test window, the winning recipe is: leakage-safe, strictly-past-only entity aggregations (RFM + velocity + risk encodings) → tuned GBDT (LightGBM/XGBoost/CatBoost) ensemble → rank-averaged blend, validated with time-based/purged walk-forward splits.** Deep tabular and graph models generally do NOT beat well-engineered GBDTs on this kind of tabular fraud task.
- **Do NOT use SMOTE/ADASYN, random K-fold CV, or test-set target statistics** — all three are actively harmful here (resampling degrades PR-AUC and calibration; K-fold leaks time) and the last two violate competition rules. Use `scale_pos_weight`/class weights + threshold-free ranking instead.

## Key Findings

### A. Dataset availability
1. **No public copy of the REACT 2026 data exists.** The competition landing page (seureact.vercel.app) and the IEEE SEU Student Branch (Southeast University, a private university in Tejgaon, Dhaka, Bangladesh, established 2002) host it only inside the private Kaggle competition. Extensive searching against the competition name, organizer, and schema returned no dataset mirror.
2. The schema (`transaction_id, customer_id, timestamp, amount, merchant_id, merchant_category, device_id, device_type, location, payment_method, transaction_type, account_age_days, fraud`) does not match any known public dataset field-for-field. It is almost certainly **generated with a custom simulator or a Faker/LLM script**, given the 2026 dates, the microsecond-precision test max (`...341114`), BDT currency, and the organizers' explicit refusal to disclose the fraud-generation process.
3. Closest analogs, ranked by structural similarity:
   - **ULB "Reproducible ML for Credit Card Fraud Detection" Handbook simulator** (Le Borgne, Siblini, Lebichot, Bontempi, Université Libre de Bruxelles / Worldline) — **BEST analog.** Raw schema is `TRANSACTION_ID, TX_DATETIME, CUSTOMER_ID, TERMINAL_ID, TX_AMOUNT, TX_FRAUD`, ~1.8M transactions, 5,000 customers, 10,000 terminals, 183 days (6 months), <1% fraud, with explicitly time-dependent fraud scenarios. This is the single most directly transferable resource — its entire feature-engineering, feedback-delay, and validation methodology maps onto REACT 2026, and it reports results in **Average Precision** (the REACT metric).
   - **IEEE-CIS / Vesta (Kaggle 2019)** — 590,540 transactions, 3.5% fraud, strict temporal train/test split; entity-ID ("card") aggregation was the key winning move. Metric was ROC-AUC not AP, but the FE philosophy transfers directly.
   - **Sparkov / "Credit Card Transactions Fraud Detection"** (Kartik Shenoy; Brandon Harris generator) — synthetic; has merchant, category, amount, timestamp, geo; ~1.85M transactions.
   - **PaySim / BankSim** (Lopez-Rojas et al.) — mobile-money simulators; schema differs (balances, step-based hourly time) and fraud typologies are widely regarded as too simplistic, but the mobile-money framing is culturally close to bKash/Nagad.
   - **Fraudulent E-Commerce Transactions** (shriyashjagtap, Kaggle; 1.4M+ rows) — notably includes `Customer ID, Transaction Amount, Payment Method, Device Used, Account Age Days, Transaction Hour` — the **closest column-name match** to REACT among real Kaggle datasets, but it is e-commerce/USD and largely single-transaction-per-user oriented.
   - **Bangladesh/BDT-specific**: no bKash/Nagad-style labeled fraud dataset with this schema exists publicly.

### B. Papers/results on matched & analogous datasets
- **ULB Handbook baselines (simulated data, TEST set):** metric is Average Precision via `sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score`. The handbook states verbatim that "a random classifier would give an AUC ROC of 0.5, and an Average Precision of 0.007 (the proportion of frauds in the test set)," and that the tree/boosting models reach much higher AP than logistic regression and shallow trees. Across the handbook's chapters the ranking is consistent: **XGBoost and Random Forest give the highest Average Precision, followed by logistic regression, then decision trees** (reported simulated-test AP values fall in roughly the 0.5–0.66 band for the top models depending on window/config; treat the ordering, not the absolute number, as the transferable finding). On their **real-world** data, "XGBoost performs significantly better than random forest and logistic regression (AUC ROC of 0.899 versus AUC ROC of 0.78 and 0.788, respectively)," and Random Forest's AP collapsed due to overfitting — a direct warning to regularize hard.
- **IEEE-CIS 1st place (Yakovlev & Deotte, "FraudSquad"):** private ROC-AUC ≈ 0.9459. Single-model public/private scores, verbatim from their writeup: "CatBoost (Public/Private LB of 0.9639/0.9408), LGBM (0.9617/0.9384), and XGB (0.9602/0.9324)." Final = a stack (LGBM trained on CAT+XGB predictions) plus an equal-weight ensemble, "post processed by taking all predictions from a single client (credit card) and replacing them with that client's average prediction." Over/undersampling did NOT help LGBM/XGB; CatBoost benefited slightly from `class_weights=[1,2.5]`.
- **IEEE-CIS top-5% (Arun Mohan):** UID from card1+D1+addr1, group aggregations, XGBoost, temporal split — "we got a Public leaderboard score of 0.9004 and 0.9238 for private LB" on the dataset where "96.5% of transactions are not fraud and the rest 3.5% ... are fraudulent."
- **Booking.com tabular transformer (Krutikov et al., arXiv:2405.13692):** one of the few results where a self-supervised pretrained FT-Transformer beat a heavily tuned LightGBM on **Average Precision**. The best-tuned LightGBM baseline scored AP "0.471 ± 0.003"; the SSL-pretrained FT-large reached AP ≈ 0.491, and the paper states SSL pretraining "outperforms heavily tuned GBDTs by a considerable margin of the Average Precision (AP) score" while requiring "significantly less annotated data (up to 40%)." **Caveat: confidential internal data + large unlabeled corpus + SSL pretraining** — high effort, conditional result.
- **GNN vs GBDT:** multiple benchmarks (insurance G-GBM paper arXiv:2510.05676; Kumo/RelBench; the cesarsotovalero survey) show plain GBDTs beat or match GNNs for single-transaction fraud; GNNs help mainly for organized "fraud rings." GBDT + graph-derived features (as tabular inputs) is the pragmatic sweet spot.
- **Tabular deep learning generally loses:** Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux (arXiv:2207.08815, NeurIPS 2022 Datasets & Benchmarks) benchmarked 45 datasets and conclude "tree-based models remain state-of-the-art on medium-sized data (∼10K samples) even without accounting for their superior speed." Shwartz-Ziv & Armon ("Tabular data: Deep learning is not all you need") agree.
- **Resampling evidence:** "The Hidden Cost of Resampling" (arXiv:2606.29720) and the online credit-card study (arXiv:2206.13152) show SMOTE/ADASYN/RUS **cut PR-AUC and wreck calibration** on tree ensembles (CatBoost: SMOTE −64% PR-AUC, ADASYN −65%; borderline SMOTE −48%). Threshold/cost-sensitive methods dominate.

## Details

### 1. PREPROCESSING (leakage-safe)
- **Sort by timestamp first**, always. Every downstream step must respect this ordering.
- **Categorical missingness** (`merchant_category, device_type, location`): do NOT mean-impute. For GBDTs, keep NaN native (LightGBM/XGBoost/CatBoost split on missing) and add an explicit `*_is_missing` binary flag — Deotte's IEEE-CIS work found missingness itself is predictive. "Missing = average" imputation is dangerous in fraud because non-average behavior is the signal.
- **High-cardinality IDs** (`customer_id, merchant_id, device_id`): never one-hot. Use (a) frequency/count encoding computed strictly on past rows, (b) leakage-safe prior-only target ("risk") encoding with Bayesian smoothing (below), and (c) CatBoost's native ordered target statistics. Treat IDs as keys for aggregation, not as direct model inputs.
- **Amount transforms**: `log1p(amount)`; "round-number" indicators (`amount % 100 == 0`, `% 500 == 0`, `% 1000 == 0` — common in BDT mobile-money fraud, and organizers referenced amounts like ৳30,000); decimal/cents part `amount - floor(amount)`; amount ratio to the customer's trailing mean/median.
- **Timestamp decomposition**: hour, day-of-week, day-of-month, is_weekend, is_night (the handbook uses TX_DURING_WEEKEND / TX_DURING_NIGHT), time-since-midnight, ISO week index. Fraud rate is known to spike in specific night hours.
- **Microsecond artifact**: strip/ignore sub-second precision as a feature (it is a generation artifact and a drift trap — and reverse-engineering it is explicitly discouraged), but you may keep a `duplicate_timestamp` flag.
- **Duplicate/near-duplicate detection**: flag identical `(customer_id, amount, merchant_id)` within small time deltas; count exact-timestamp collisions per entity.

### 2. TIME-AWARE, LEAKAGE-SAFE FEATURE ENGINEERING (the heart)
All features for a transaction at time *t* use only rows strictly before *t*. You MAY compute non-target aggregations over earlier test-period rows (allowed by the rules); you may NEVER use the fraud label of the current or any future row, and target-based encodings must additionally respect a **feedback delay** (see risk encoding).

- **RFM aggregates** (per customer / merchant / device / location), sliding windows 1h, 6h, 24h, 7d, 30d + expanding: count of transactions, sum/mean/std/median/min/max of amount. Implement with pandas `groupby(entity).rolling(window, on=timestamp)` on the time-sorted frame — this is exactly the handbook's `get_customer_spending_behaviour_features` with windows [1,7,30] days, producing e.g. `CUSTOMER_ID_NB_TX_{1,7,30}DAY_WINDOW` and `CUSTOMER_ID_AVG_AMOUNT_{1,7,30}DAY_WINDOW`.
- **Velocity/burst**: number of txns by entity in last 1h/6h/24h; time-since-last-transaction per customer/device/merchant; inter-transaction time mean/std; ratio of last-1h count to trailing-30d hourly average (captures automation/bursts). This is the same time-delta + count philosophy that won TalkingData AdTracking.
- **Deviation/z-scores**: `(amount − trailing_mean)/trailing_std` per customer; ratio-to-rolling-mean; amount percentile within the customer's own history.
- **First-seen/novelty**: is this the first time this customer used this device/merchant/location; days-since-first-seen for each entity pair; count of distinct prior devices for the customer.
- **Entity-sharing / fan-out (causal)**: distinct customers per device (so far), distinct devices per customer, distinct merchants per customer, distinct locations per device — all cumulative-past-only. High device→customer fan-out is a classic account-takeover / fraud-ring signal (Sam's Club/industry FE writeups list "number of unique devices per user id ... and vice versa" as top features).
- **Impossible-travel / location-change**: flag when consecutive transactions for a customer switch coarse location within a short window; count location switches in last 24h.
- **Behavioral entropy**: Shannon entropy of a customer's historical distribution over merchant_category / location / hour (low entropy = habitual; a sudden entropy jump = anomaly).
- **Account-age interactions**: `account_age_days` × amount, × velocity; a "new account + high amount + new device" composite; new-account fraud is a documented typology.
- **Leakage-safe risk (target) encoding**: prior-only mean fraud rate per merchant_category/location/device_type with Bayesian smoothing `r̂_v = (n_v·r_v + m·r̄)/(n_v + m)`, m ≈ 20–30, AND a **feedback delay**. The handbook uses a 7-day delay: the risk for day N uses labels only up to day N−7, mimicking real investigation lag and preventing look-ahead. Compute out-of-fold within a time-respecting scheme; never compute target stats on test. Audit rule: if a target-encoded feature suddenly tops importance and CV jumps, suspect leakage and recompute out-of-fold — "most of the gain usually evaporates."
- **IEEE-CIS "UID/magic" caveat (compliance-critical)**: Deotte's win came from constructing a client UID (card1+addr1+D1) and aggregating on it. This generalizes to REACT as **customer_id/device_id behavioral aggregation** — legitimate and encouraged. BUT the rules discourage "targeting entity IDs" (i.e., uniquely fingerprinting/deanonymizing a held-out entity to infer its outcome) and reverse-engineering the generator. Keep aggregations **behavioral and past-only**; do not build a feature whose sole purpose is to identify a specific test entity's label. UID *aggregations* = fine; UID-as-label-lookup = non-compliant and drift-fragile.
- **Graph/relational**: build bipartite customer–device / customer–merchant graphs; compute past-only node degree, distinct-neighbor counts, connected-component size, PageRank, Louvain community as tabular features fed to GBDT. Full GNNs (GraphSAGE, R-GCN, CARE-GNN, PC-GNN, TGN) rarely beat GBDT + graph-features on tabular single-transaction fraud and add compliance/leakage risk; use them only if time permits and validate strictly. The leakage-safe temporal-graph-feature paper (arXiv:2603.06632) shows a hybrid Random Forest + causal graph descriptors reaching validation AP 0.925 but dropping to test AP 0.537 under temporal shift — a vivid illustration of the drift you'll face.
- **Sequence models**: per-customer transaction-history LSTM/GRU/Transformer (TabFormer-style), optionally self-supervised pretraining. Only Booking.com-style SSL has beaten GBDT on AP, and only with a large unlabeled corpus. Treat as a secondary ensemble member, not the core.

### 3. MODELING
- **Core: GBDT trio** — LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost.
- **LightGBM starting ranges**: `objective=binary`, `metric=average_precision` (or `auc`), `learning_rate` 0.01–0.05, `num_leaves` 31–255, `min_child_samples` 50–500, `feature_fraction` 0.5–0.9, `bagging_fraction` 0.6–0.9, `lambda_l1/l2` 0–10, `scale_pos_weight` ≈ (neg/pos) ≈ 50–65 for 1.5–2% fraud (tune 0.2–2× that), large `n_estimators` + early stopping on a time-based validation set.
- **XGBoost**: `max_depth` 4–8, `eta` 0.01–0.05, `subsample`/`colsample_bytree` 0.6–0.9, `min_child_weight` 1–10, `scale_pos_weight` as above, `eval_metric=aucpr`.
- **CatBoost**: native categorical handling (pass IDs/categoricals directly), `depth` 6–8, tune `l2_leaf_reg`, ordered boosting, `auto_class_weights=Balanced` or manual `class_weights` (the IEEE-CIS winner used `[1,2.5]`).
- **Cost-sensitive / focal loss**: prefer `scale_pos_weight`/class weights over resampling; a focal-loss custom objective can help ranking under extreme imbalance.
- **Anomaly-detection hybrids**: Isolation Forest / autoencoder reconstruction error as *extra features* into the GBDT (not standalone) — modest gains.
- **Deep tabular (TabNet, FT-Transformer, SAINT, NODE)**: benchmark for ensemble diversity but expect them to underperform tuned GBDT per Grinsztajn et al.

### 4. CLASS IMBALANCE — what actually works
- **Recommended**: `scale_pos_weight`/class weights + threshold-free ranking. Because AP rewards ranking, you rarely need a hard decision threshold at all. Optionally BalancedBagging/EasyEnsemble of GBDTs for diversity.
- **Avoid**: SMOTE/ADASYN/random over/undersampling — evidence shows they cut PR-AUC (CatBoost SMOTE −64%, ADASYN −65% AP) and destroy probability calibration; they only inflate recall/F1 at the cost of the exact metric you're scored on. If you must recalibrate after any resampling, use isotonic/Platt — but better not to resample at all.

### 5. VALIDATION (mirror the 2-month future test after 6.5-month train)
- **No random K-fold.** Use **time-based walk-forward / expanding-window** CV. Concretely: train Jan–Apr → validate May; train Jan–May → validate Jun; train Jan–Jun → validate first half of Jul. Your final local holdout should be the **last ~2 months of train** (≈ mid-May → mid-Jul) to mimic the 2-month test span (test runs 2026-07-16 → 2026-09-15).
- **Purged & embargoed CV (López de Prado)**: insert a gap/embargo (≥7 days, matching the feedback delay used in risk features) between train and validation folds so rolling-window features don't straddle the boundary.
- **Client/time consistency check (Deotte)**: also validate on unseen customers (split by customer_id) to ensure features generalize to new entities, since the test set will contain new customers.
- **Avoid public-LB overfitting**: trust your time-based local CV over the 60% public LB; the 40% private LB decides the winner. Track CV↔LB correlation and prefer submissions that are strong on both.

### 6. CONCEPT DRIFT (organizers explicitly warn of it)
- **Adversarial validation**: train a classifier to distinguish train vs test rows (using only raw non-target features); AUC ≫ 0.5 ⇒ drift. Use it to (a) drop/deweight drift-inducing features and (b) select validation rows most similar to test. (See arXiv:2112.10078 for the credit-scoring formulation.)
- **Recency weighting**: weight training samples by recency (higher weight to later dates) so the model tilts toward the near-future test distribution.
- **Drift-robust feature selection**: prefer features stable across the train timeline; drop features whose distribution shifts most (ranked by adversarial-validation importance).
- Optionally: ensemble-of-eras (train separate models on time chunks and blend), or sliding-window retraining.

### 7. CALIBRATION & POST-PROCESSING (AP = ranking metric)
- Because AP depends only on ranking, **rank-averaging** ensembles (average of per-model rank percentiles) is often more robust than probability averaging across heterogeneous models — a common choice in top fraud solutions.
- **Blending/stacking**: equal-weight blend of LGBM+XGB+CAT, or a stacker (LGBM on OOF predictions) as the IEEE-CIS winners did; keep the stacker's OOF strictly time-respecting.
- **Seed averaging**: average 3–5 seeds per model to cut variance.
- **Entity post-processing**: the IEEE-CIS winners replaced all predictions for a UID with that UID's mean. Powerful, but only valid if the entity recurs and the rules permit; for REACT, a milder version (blend a transaction's score toward its customer's mean predicted score) may help ranking — but test for leakage/compliance first.
- Isotonic/Platt calibration only matters if you need true probabilities; for AP ranking it is optional.

### 8. WINNING KAGGLE SOLUTIONS (patterns to copy)
- **IEEE-CIS 1st (FraudSquad)**: UID aggregation + GBDT trio + stack/blend + entity averaging → private ROC-AUC 0.9459.
- **IEEE-CIS top-5% (Arun Mohan)**: UID from card1+D1+addr1, group aggregations, XGBoost, temporal split → private 0.9238.
- **American Express Default (customer-sequence aggregation)**: aggregate transaction-level → customer-level with mean/std/min/max/last + diff/lag features; the last statement per customer is the most predictive row; GBDT (LightGBM) core, GRU/Transformer as ensemble members; multiple write-ups report stacking gave minimal lift and FE dominated (e.g., aggregations "increased model accuracy by ~12%"; Deotte's 15th-place used a Transformer distilled from LightGBM).
- **Home Credit Default Risk**: time-aware aggregation across historical tables → maps to per-entity rolling aggregations here.
- **TalkingData AdTracking (click fraud)**: time-delta + count/aggregation features per entity (IP/app/device) in rolling windows — the same velocity philosophy.

## Recommendations (staged)

**Stage 0 — Setup & validation harness (do first).** Sort by timestamp. Build the time-based walk-forward CV + a mid-May→mid-Jul local holdout. Run adversarial validation to quantify train↔test drift *before* any modeling. Benchmark: a bare LightGBM on raw columns + `scale_pos_weight` gives your baseline AP; everything is measured against it. *Threshold to change course:* if the public LB and your local holdout AP diverge sharply, your validation is wrong — fix it before tuning anything.

**Stage 1 — Feature engineering (biggest lever).** Implement, in expected-ROI order: (1) timestamp features + amount transforms; (2) per-customer RFM + velocity + z-score features over [1h,6h,24h,7d,30d]; (3) first-seen/novelty + fan-out (distinct-neighbor) features; (4) leakage-safe smoothed risk encoding with a 7-day feedback delay for merchant_category/location/device_type; (5) entropy + impossible-travel; (6) optional graph-derived tabular features. Re-measure AP after each block; keep only blocks that improve time-based CV. *Threshold:* if a block doesn't improve holdout AP, drop it (likely noise or drift-fragile).

**Stage 2 — Modeling & tuning.** Tune the GBDT trio with the ranges above; early-stop on the time-based validation. Add IsolationForest/autoencoder scores as features. Only with spare time, add one sequence model (GRU over customer history) or GBDT+graph features for ensemble diversity. *Threshold:* adopt a deep/graph model only if it beats the GBDT on your out-of-time holdout AP, not just on random split.

**Stage 3 — Ensemble & submit.** Seed-average each model (3–5 seeds), then rank-average the trio. Compare an equal-weight blend vs a time-respecting LGBM stacker; pick by local holdout AP. Apply recency sample weighting if adversarial validation showed strong drift. Choose final submissions to maximize private-LB robustness (trust CV over public LB).

**Compliance guardrails (verify before every submission):**
- ❌ No external data of any kind (no bKash/Nagad datasets, no embeddings pretrained on other transaction data — general-purpose backbones only).
- ❌ No random K-fold; only time-based / purged / walk-forward.
- ❌ No fitting encoders/scalers/target-stats on test.csv (raw, past-only, non-target aggregation over test rows IS allowed).
- ❌ No use of current/future fraud labels; enforce the feedback delay on all target-derived features.
- ❌ Don't reverse-engineer the generator or build features that uniquely fingerprint a held-out entity to look up its outcome — entity *aggregations* are fine, entity *deanonymization* is not.

## Caveats
- The dataset identification is a **negative result held with high confidence**, but it is inherently unverifiable without the organizers confirming the generator. Treat the ULB Handbook as the closest transferable template, not an identical dataset.
- All PR-AUC/AP numbers cited are from **analogous** datasets (ULB simulated, Booking.com, online credit-card), not REACT 2026 — absolute AP on REACT will differ; use them to rank methods, not to set a target. The ULB simulated-test AP is quoted differently across handbook chapters/configs (a top figure of ~0.496 appears in the Chapter-3 baseline text, while other tables/windows report higher values up to ~0.66); rely on the *ordering* (XGBoost/RF ≫ LR ≫ shallow trees), not the point estimate.
- The Booking.com transformer result is a **preprint on confidential internal data**; treat "transformers beat GBDT on AP" as conditional (large unlabeled corpus + SSL), not general.
- The IEEE-CIS metric was ROC-AUC, not AP; the FE transfers but the exact ensembling/post-processing choices should be re-validated under AP.
- The 2nd-place IEEE-CIS exact private score/architecture could not be verified from a citable rendered source (the Kaggle writeup is JS-gated); the CPMP-view writeup URL is https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/writeups/2-uncles-and-3-puppies-2nd-solution-cpmp-view
